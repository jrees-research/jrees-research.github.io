Truth inference over ICD-11 diagnoses

  • Bayesian hierarchical models
  • PyMC
  • ICD-11
  • Simulation

When clinicians and AI systems diagnose the same patient, they often disagree, and there is usually no verified diagnosis to use as a ground truth. In this project, we aim to simultaneously infer the most likely true diagnosis and the reliability of each diagnoser in the absence of ground truth labels, using a hierarchical Bayesian model.

The model leverages the structure of the ICD-11 classification of diseases, in which diseases are arranged hierarchically in a directed acyclic graph (DAG). A diagnosis that is close to the true diagnosis in the ICD-11 DAG is treated as a less severe error than one in a distant part of the DAG. The output is a posterior probability distribution over diagnoses, which shows how uncertain the inferred diagnosis is, together with an estimate of each diagnoser's reliability.

Status

  • Method developed and evaluated on simulated data
  • Paper under review
  • Short talk presented at the Wits Science Faculty research showcase, where it was awarded second place for Best Research Presentation
  • Oral presentation at the SASA 2026 conference

Next steps

  • Application to real data from studies based in Johannesburg public hospitals
  • Investigation of non-taxonomic distance measures, especially those representing symptom presentation similarity and/or diagnostic confusability

Antimicrobial resistance (AMR) surveillance and modelling

  • Antimicrobial resistance
  • Sepsis
  • Probabilistic modelling

Clinicians treating sepsis usually have to choose antibiotics days before laboratory results confirm which organism is present and which antibiotics it is resistant to. In this project, we aim to use clinical data from sepsis patients in Johannesburg public hospitals to (1) describe organism prevalence and antimicrobial susceptibility patterns, (2) develop predictive models for identifying antimicrobial resistance and predicting mortality based on patient and hospital features, and (3) work towards optimising empiric antimicrobial therapy based on these predictions.

Status

  • Data is being analysed in collaboration with clinicians

Next steps

  • Consider probabilistic models using longitudinal data to predict mortality and resistance
  • Consider the analysis and modelling of high-frequency data from wearable devices

Sequential decision support in internal medicine and paediatrics

  • Bayesian decision theory
  • Value of information
  • Causal graphs
  • Longitudinal data

This project is related to the VALID studies (see the first paper from colleagues). We intend to use longitudinal, multimodal patient data from South African hospitals to build statistically transparent and calibrated clinical decision-support tools for diagnosis and patient management.

Current directions of inquiry include causal graphs of the clinical workflow with and without AI assistance, and methods for choosing the next investigation by its expected value of information.
